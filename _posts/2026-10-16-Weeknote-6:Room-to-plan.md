A short update this week as lots of travelling and workshops. This week I attended Strategic Room Planning ("SRP"), we released the Single Point of Access ("SPoA") Minimum Viable Product ("MVP") changes, I worked on criteria for establishing product gates in our governance, and I officially started feeding into the transformation strategy. (sorry for all the accronyms!)

Strategic Room Planning (SRP) is a two-day workshop where senior leaders across Products and Platforms come together to align on strategy and do more detailed, joined-up planning. It was great to see more user-centred vision work from the NHS App, NHS Online and Prevention teams, bringing to life a near-future view of what care could look like. Innovation is so complicated in this environment that it's hard to decide how far ahead any vision work should reach (6 months - 3 years). I think they landed at a reasonable, tangible distance from where we are now, presenting some of the initiatives we're already working on in a compelling, user-centred way.

## Product governance 

As part of establishing best practices in our Portfolio I have developed a few tools to be applied:
- A product team maturity assessment to help us score and rank the MDT teams and drive up maturity from the perspective of continuous user research, evidence based decision-making, alignment to outcomes etc. 
- A stage-gate UCD assessment tool based on the GDS and <a href="https://service-manual.nhs.uk/standards-and-technology/service-standard" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">NHSE Service Standard</a> principles, this will enable us to standardise how we evaluate Discovery, Alpha and Beta outputs, empower decision and traceability. There is an process established in NHS App for design crits and assurance and as much as possible it would be good to borrow from those established practices. 

I've also been thinking about a term I keep coming across: <a href="https://www.welcometothejungle.com/en/articles/cal-newport-slow-productivity-hustle-culture" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">slow productivity</a>. It feels especially pertinent now that AI tools are significantly increasing teams' output and creating a different type of work. As AI is used more and more commonly in our day-to-day you can easily spot it, it looks good but requires a lot of unpicking and tightening of language. I came across the term <a href="https://fs.blog/slop-grenade/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">slop grenade</a> on LinkedIn describing when someopne sends you a lot of AI output without reviewing it themselves.

I had a great chat with a colleague on the train back from Leeds about the history of GDS and what it has achieved, policy labs and policy design, and the difference between theorists and people who deliver real change - it's was really refreshing and motivating to hear.

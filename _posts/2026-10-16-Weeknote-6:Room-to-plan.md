This week I attended Strategic Room Planning, we released the SPoA MVP changes, I worked on criteria for establishing product gates in our governance, and I officially started feeding into the transformation strategy.

Strategic Room Planning (SRP) is a two-day workshop where senior leaders across Products and Platforms come together to align on strategy and do more detailed, joined-up planning.

It was great to see more user-centred vision work from the NHS App, NHS Online and Prevention teams, bringing to life a near-future view of what care could look like. Innovation is so complicated in this environment that it's hard to decide how far ahead any vision work should reach. I think they landed at a reasonable distance from where we are now, presenting some of the initiatives we're already working on in a compelling, user-centred way.

I've also been thinking about a term I keep coming across: <a href="https://www.welcometothejungle.com/en/articles/cal-newport-slow-productivity-hustle-culture" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">slow productivity</a>. It feels especially pertinent now that AI tools are significantly increasing teams' output and creating a different type of work.

I had a great chat with Jessica Ferguson on the train back from Leeds about the history of GDS and what it has achieved, policy labs and policy design, and the difference between theorists and people who can deliver real change.

My boss returned to work this week and, somewhat unexpectedly, it triggered a worry that I hadn't delivered to expectations. A useful reminder that the listen-and-understand phase doesn't always feel productive, even when it is.

I'm also finding collaboration with peers on a shared workstream harder than expected. Building trust across Lead PMs takes time, and I'm working on it.

## App-led or API-led and eating your own dog food

I met with Rourke, Head of Transformation and Onboarding, to discuss our API strategy and the interesting dichotomy between being App-led or API-led. Right now the API team are often playing catch-up with the Professional App team, but to be truly API-first: the Professional App should be a *customer* of the API. This is where the term "eating your own dog food" comes in and it's relevant. 

| | Pro App first | API first |
|---|---|---|
| **Pros** | We control changes, instant rollout to users | Users work in a single interface via their preferred system, fewer manual transcription errors, more efficient, less burden |
| **Cons** | Users juggle a separate app alongside their EPR, the API falls behind | Suppliers must uplift their integrations, so rollout is slower and depends on them |

In my days working in Financial Services we were very API Driven and there are many learnings to apply here in terms of being API first, using your own UI to design and test new features and improving our developer onboarding experiences. 

## NHS Online: a Trojan Horse?

I also met the <a href="https://www.england.nhs.uk/digitaltechnology/nhs-online/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">NHS Online</a> team, a fascinating new proposition. Their scoped approach, starting with a limited set of conditions, reminded me of the Pharmacy First rollout having a distinct number of treatments that they could take ownership of.

With my GPIT hat on, I was immediately curious which EPR would power this new virtual trust: Epic, Nervecentre or Cerner? The answer was none of the above. They're building greenfield. Could this be the Trojan Horse that grows into the first national, clinician-facing EPR?

## Transformation strategy

I'm not usually a fan of teams disappearing into a black hole and emerging with a strategy for everyone to organise around, but I also really respect that you need to protect teams from noise and to do any real innovation you have to break away from the existing landscape. There is a good leader on this and she seems to be able to cut-through the noise and is orchestrating a good transition - I wonder how I can contribute more? 

## More chats

Good conversations with more of the SLT team who gave me a useful steer on becoming more of a thought leader and SME in this Referrals and Appointments space, which was great to hear as it’s easy to get lumbered with the day to day “fire-fighting” and lose your strategic perspective. 

I've also been reflecting on how I create an environment where teams can thrive. Honestly, it's not something I massively prioritised enough in the past, I have always been very results driven and in the past this has sometimes come at the expense of team dynamics. I'm reading *Leaders Eat Last* by Simon Sinek, and sitting next to the Prevention team, I'm thinking about which of their good practices we could borrow. It’s a more healthy place to exist.

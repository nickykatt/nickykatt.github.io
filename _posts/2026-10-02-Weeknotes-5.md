Focus this week to nail a concise weeknotes that I can finish off efficiently on Friday. 

Another busy week, including another round of careful management on a controversial product feature, opportunity sizing and benefits, and a brilliant chat with a Product coach who helped me recalibrate my focus again into organisational outcomes. 

### Stick or Twist?

Our portfolio has some really big ambitions and numbers forecasted to hit over the next few years until 2030. Although this is very motivating from an impact perspective, it also means we are under a lot of pressure and scrutiny from senior executives. We have weekly updates with some of the most senior exec and, as I’m sure is common in most companies, they tend to have very specific detailed and often technical feedback. This can often be challenging for the teams as we are in a weekly cycle of preparing exec updates which must be carefully crafted to ensure information is shared in an honest and constructive way. 

The development team is in a sticky situation for a number of reasons and now must carefully manoeuvre this feature back into a viable position, carefully mitigating a number of risks. The feature is the result of a chain of hard decisions over the last year and is now at the very end of a line where tough decisions must be made balancing risk, user value and organisational value. 

However, it’s great to see the team, under pressure, all pitch in and show how productive we can be working in an agile way as a truly multi-disciplinary unit. 

### Opportunity Sizing

I enjoy the practice of opportunity mapping and being entrepreneurial as a product manager to spot opportunities and create small “fag packet” calculations to identify opportunities to pursue - it feels creative and really motivates you with the potential impact you could have. 

I remember, early in my career, when it finally clicked that most things, with some simple calculations and evidence based research could be quantified relatively easily. It really forces you to think about the human costs. 

As part of my practice, I want to encourage the PMs to be more creative and entrepreneurial to spot these opportunities, quickly build an opportunity model and then submit these as beta for our portfolio to commit to exploring. In the past, especially in financial services, we had some really good tools and support to do this. It’s really helpful to have a book that contained your base metrics like the average salary of different staff, total numbers of staff, total number of practices and product data like referral volume or volume of key parts of the process. In an ideal world we would have a really robust representative time and motion study of all of our user activities that we can use to baseline against. These numbers and tools massively help when quickly trying to sketch up a new opportunity case. 

I will create this tool and establish a good practice process with the team. 

### Product focus 

This week I had my first proper session with a Product Coach, Roberta, which was really useful. I’m always on the hunt for good product counterparts that I can connect with and discuss work but it was really refreshing and hugely valuable to take a step back and talk in a slightly more selfish way about my experience and my goals. 

We both have a range of experience working for different sized organisations outside of the public-sector and Roberta quickly identified something that I have been wrestling with during my time here, how to recalibrate my focus and expectations coming from fast-paced, results driven organisations. I’m used to shipping new products and services and getting them into the hands of users and that’s my perception of success, whereas here we’re playing a different game and you have to spend more time focussing on the improving the conditions for teams and creating an environment where complex, rainbow teams are able to thrive. 

We discussed increasing focus on internal outputs, such as creating processes, models and tools to improve our product capability. Although this is aligned to my objectives and a big part of what is described as Head of Product capabilities and above, I often default to focussing on the product and hard benefits. 

It was a good reminder and I prioritised more time on my Thursday afternoon to looking into our Product Governance workstream, particularly coming up with a strategy to establish a more assumption-driven product process, including the right level of product governance through key SME reviews and proportionate stage-gate assurance - I usually have an immediate allergic reaction to any heavy processes but in this environment it’s necessary and we have to find the right balance. 

Again, this does come down to a reset around how we do product with our suppliers but ultimately this is about responsible management of public investment in services that have the best chance of delivering outcomes to the frontline. 



It’s good to think of the product lifecycle as mini-investment gates, do we have enough confidence to continue to invest in this opportunity? What cost-effective tests can we run and evidence can we gather to validate some of our riskiest assumptions?

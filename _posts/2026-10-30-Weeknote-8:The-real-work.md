This week we held our policy workshop to explore how we can be more joined up with our Elective policy colleagues. We're also working up technical options papers to take to leadership as we navigate our way into transformation.

### The policy workshop

We ran a great workshop on Tuesday, getting closer to our colleagues in Elective and understanding their views on the rules, incentives and practices currently in place around clinician messaging, Advice and Guidance (A&G), and administration.

We discussed some of the headline metrics: Who feels most on the hook for RTT and the 18-week constitutional standard for time to treatment, the referrer or the service provider? How does that break down into the 5-day A&G metric and the time to first decision?

If we can align on the policy outcomes, can we also align on digital experiments to see which are most effective at driving behaviour change and delivering our joint outcomes? It's really exciting to think that this work could affect all of us at such critical moments in our lives.

### Reducing the noise

We also spoke a lot about noise: how much communication happens because patients go through their GP to find out where their hospital appointment is. I came across this often in the user research we did in General Practice. GPs are used as a proxy to navigate secondary care, and secondary care relies on General Practice to manage patients and help them navigate their care journey.

In an ideal world, and hopefully in the not-too-distant future, platforms like e-RS and Wayfinder will make it clear to patients exactly where they are in their journey, removing a lot of this noise from the system. (The noise is the problem, not the patient care.)

Clinician messaging should be protected for queries that affect patient care and improve outcomes as efficiently as possible.

### Questions we're still exploring

- What are the hard and soft rules around incentives and penalties, and what happens when they're broken?
- How do clinicians see their own performance?
- What other ideas could influence behaviour change? Could we work with behavioural insights teams?

### From one end of the spectrum to the other

This is really exciting for me. After 2.5 years of frontline management of the GPIT EPR suppliers, I'm now at the other end of the spectrum, working directly with policymakers. I've found myself on the biggest, most complex national product, at the crux of real transformational change, with policy colleagues engaged and a genuine opportunity to do something disruptive.

### Who owns what

Teams here can be disparate. In private sector product roles I had much more direct control over things like benefits and opportunity cases. Here, those responsibilities are spread across several teams, so a big part of the job is joining them up. 

Meanwhile, I'm writing a paper for transformation and trying to wrestle it into shape...
